About Me

I am a fourth-year student in the Rochester Institute of Technology's Game Design and Development accelerated BS/MS program. Levelsmith Labs: Foundry 2D

RIT Entrepreneurial Co-op
Summer 2026

Worked with a team of twelve as a game developer.

A level designing tool created for rapid prototyping purposes of 2D platformer levels. This tool was created to allow users to create their own 2D platformer levels with customizable enemy properties, player properties, and assets. I created and implemented all asset swapping capabilities to allow the user to change their sprites, animations, and audio assets to whatever they like.

This project was easily the most professional experience I have had with game development. We were a team of twelve total, split into a design team and development team with team leads and one lead producer. We utilized task boards and large amounts of documentation to keep everyone on the same page and better prepare future teams that might work on the project.

Class Projects

Scraplight

Class: Game Design and Development II
Fall 2025

Created by team 5 Devs in a Trenchcoat

As a semester long project, we created a game about avoiding a light-seeking monster while trying to escape a factory. Created in Unity 3D, we had a big focus on the lighting and atmosphere of our game, as well as making a monster that felt scary without being too strong. The player is given multiple options for avoiding the killer, like toggling their flashlight, throwing flares, or using other lights in the environment, as well as the ability to find keys to unlock doors, opening more areas for the player to explore.

This class had a major focus on being as similar to a real-world game development as possible. We each had roles on our team, used Jira to help manage our project, and organized our work progress into multiple sprints over the course of the semester. I was one of two main programmers on the team, working across many aspects of our game like player and camera movement, interactable items like flares, keys, and doors, as well as the UI elements of our game, like the HUD, pausing, and all the different screens the player might see. My biggest contribution, and the one I am most proud of, is the monster AI. I created the monster and programmed all of its behaviors, having it react to lights, searching for the player, and having multiple different behavior states that it transitions through depending on the environment surrounding it.

Personal Projects

Calculape

GMTK Game Jam 2026
July 2026

Created with five other RIT Students

Given the theme "countdown" and four days to make a game, we decided on a simple game in which you play as a monkey that has to press values on a screen in a descending order. As the player progresses, there are more themes on the buttons that represent values that the player must keep track of, such as roman numerals, time on a clock, or amount of apples in a picture. For this jam, we wanted to put a large focus on keeping the gameplay simple and giving the game a lot of juice. We added sound effects, screen shake, small distractions, and shaders all based around the core gameplay of counting down.

I worked primarily as a programmer on the game. I created the tutorial for the player to learn the game, including the scrolling textboxes and scripted events. I also did a lot of implementations and making sure everything worked together smoothly.

Clueless Wizard

RIT Game Dev Club Spring 2026 Game Jam
February 2026

Created with two other RIT Students

For this game jam, we were given the theme "clueless" and around 48 hours to create a game relating to that. We decided to make a game following a wizard who is clueless about what he is doing. He has very little control over his wand and does not know how to cast spells correctly. He must make it through rooms of angry mushrooms as he casts totally randomized spells with many different effects and combinations.

For this jam, I worked as both a programmer and artist. My programming work focused on the mechanics of the spells being cast, and how the different aspects of the spells were randomly selected, including the damage, effect, spread pattern, size, and projectile speed. I also created most of the art for the game.

Wreck-King

Roll Your Fate Game Jam
November 2025

Created by my brother, David Poore, and I.

Given the chance to roll multiple dice to determine what the criteria would be for our game, we rolled the theme "time is a resource", the adventure genre, one button input type, and black and white art style. To fit these criteria, we created a game in which the player must launch themselves as a wrecking ball into buildings and obstacles to try to destroy them within a certain time limit. The space bar is the only button used throughout the game, even for navigating menus.

For this jam, both of us split up the work pretty evenly, him focusing on the player controlling and level design and me focusing on obstacles, menu navigation, and art. For this jam, we once again did all of the asset production which I drew all of the art for.
